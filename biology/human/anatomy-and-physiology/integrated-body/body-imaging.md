
# Body Imaging

**Imaging** is a vital part of diagnosing illness, understanding disease and evaluating treatments. Modern techniques providing highly detailed information have largely replaced surgery as a method of investigation.

The invention of X-Ray made the development of non-invasive medicine possible. Previously, many disorders could only be found after major surgery.

Computerized imaging allows doctors to make early diagnoses, often greatly increasing the chances of recovery. Computers process and enhance raw data, for example rendering shades of gray from an X-ray as colors.

Sometimes direct observation is essential. Viewing techniques have become less invasive with the development of instruments such as the endoscope.

## Microscopy

**Light microscopy (LM)**: light is passed through a section of material and lenses magnify the view up to 2,000 times.

**Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)**: light runs across a specimen coated with gold film. Electrons bounce of the surface, creating a 3D image. It allows for much higher magnifications than light microscopy.

## X-ray

X-rays are similar to light waves, but of very short wavelength. When passed through the body they create shadow images on photographic film. Dense structures such as bone show up white; soft tissues appear as shades of gray. To show up hollow or fluid filled structures, these are filled with a *contrast medium* that absorbs X-rays.

**Angiogram**: a special type of X-ray where a contrast medium, that shows up red, is injected into the arteries of the shoulder, neck and lower head. Bones show up white.

## CT Scanning

**Computerised Tomography (CT)**: an X-ray scanner is used with a computer to build up cross-sectional images of tissues of different density.

## MRI Scanning

**Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)**: magnets are used to line up atoms in the body, then radio waves throw the atoms out of alignment. As they realign, the atoms emit signals that are used to create an image.

## Radionuclide and PET Scanning

**Radionuclide imaging**: a radioactive chemical is injected into the body and is absorbed by the tissues. As the substance decays it emits gamma rays, which a computer forms into an image.

**Positron Emission Tomography (PET)**: is a type of radionuclide scanning that uses injected chemicals which emit radioactive particles called positrons. PET scans show function rather than anatomy.

## Ultrasound

High frequency sound waves emitted by a device called a transducer pass into the body and echo back as electrical signals. A computer processes the signals to create images.

## Endoscopes

**Endoscopes** are flexible or rigid tubes inserted into the body to view its interior, perform surgical procedures, or both. They carry a light source and instruments may be passed down them.

## Electrical Activity

Sensor pads applied to the skin detect electrical activity in muscles and nerves. The signals are displayed as a trace line. This technique includes electrocardiography (ECG) of the heart.
